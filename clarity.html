The Problem

Academic information is scattered everywhere

Students struggle to manage academic work across multiple tools, leading to confusion, missed deadlines, and lack of clarity.

Students don't struggle because they are careless. Assignments come through LMS portals. Deadlines are mentioned in class. Reminders come through WhatsApp groups. Attendance is tracked separately. Progress is rarely visualized.

This fragmentation creates missed deadlines, lack of prioritization, mental overload, and no clear sense of progress. Tools like Notion and Google Calendar help partially but require constant manual setup and switching between apps.

The real problem is not productivity. It is visibility and structured clarity.

Why This Matters

The real cost of fragmentation

When academic information is scattered, it doesn't just cause inconvenience — it has measurable consequences on students' performance and well-being:

  • Missed deadlines — Assignments and due dates spread across portals, group chats, and emails slip through the cracks, directly affecting grades.
  • Increased stress — Managing multiple disconnected tools demands constant mental overhead, raising anxiety around academic responsibilities.
  • Lack of visibility on progress — Without a unified view, students cannot accurately assess where they stand, making it hard to prioritize and stay motivated.
My Role

Research & Insights

What students actually said

Before jumping into design, I spoke with several students to understand how they currently track assignments, deadlines, and academic progress. The conversations revealed consistent patterns around tool overload and information loss.

  • Most use a mix of Google Calendar, task managers, Notion, or WhatsApp self-notes
  • There is no single source of truth
  • Students miss deadlines not because they ignore them, but because information loses visibility over time
  • Progress is rarely visualized clearly
  • Attendance tracking is completely separate from assignment tracking

Key Insights

Insight 1 — Students switch between 3–5 different tools daily to stay on top of academics. Each context switch adds cognitive load and increases the chance of missing something important.

Insight 2 — Deadlines are not forgotten — they lose visibility. Notifications fade, calendar events get buried, and group messages scroll out of sight. The problem is information architecture, not student effort.

Insight 3 — When students can't see their overall progress, they either overestimate how on track they are or feel overwhelmed without knowing why. A clear progress view reduces both false confidence and unnecessary anxiety.

Students don't need more tools. They need better visibility, prioritization, and a centralized overview.

Solution Approach

Why this solution works

The core insight was simple: students don't need another feature — they need everything they already have to be in one place, clearly organized. Clarity was designed around this principle.

Rather than adding new complexity, Clarity consolidates the most critical academic signals — assignments, attendance, and progress — into a single dashboard. The solution works because it addresses behavior rather than just functionality:

  • It reduces switching cost. When all information lives in one place, students don't have to context-switch between tools. Less switching means less dropped information.
  • It makes deadlines impossible to ignore. By surfacing upcoming assignments directly on the dashboard, the system keeps urgency visible at all times without relying on notifications alone.
  • It turns invisible progress into a motivator. Showing completion streaks and subject-level progress gives students a tangible sense of forward movement — which research shows improves sustained engagement.
  • It respects existing behavior. Instead of forcing students to adopt an entirely new workflow, Clarity is designed to slot into their existing academic habits without a steep learning curve.
Design Decisions

Why I made specific choices

Why a dashboard approach?

The dashboard was chosen as the entry point because it best matches how students actually think about their day: "What do I need to do today?" A dashboard delivers an immediate, at-a-glance answer rather than requiring navigation. It reduces the time-to-value and ensures the most critical information is never more than one click away.

Why a simplified layout?

Students already feel overwhelmed by information overload. Adding more UI elements would recreate the very problem Clarity aims to solve. A simplified layout — with limited color, clear whitespace, and focused content areas — ensures the interface itself doesn't become another source of mental clutter. Less visual noise means faster comprehension.

Why clear visual hierarchy?

In a productivity tool, hierarchy is function. When everything looks equally important, nothing is. Clear typographic hierarchy and deliberate use of color ensure that urgent items (deadlines, low attendance) stand out from supporting information (settings, history). This lets students act on what matters without scanning through noise.

Key Features

The final product

Five key sections designed to reduce mental friction and improve visibility rather than add new features. Each screen serves a deliberate purpose tied directly to a student pain point identified in research.

Dashboard

The central hub of the experience. Rather than making students navigate to find status updates, the dashboard surfaces everything critical upfront: upcoming assignments, completion progress, and current academic standing. It answers "Where am I right now?" at a glance.

Assignment Management

Directly addresses the core pain point of scattered deadline information. By bringing assignment creation, status tracking, and filtering into one dedicated view, students no longer need to cross-reference multiple apps to know what's due and when.

Progress Tracking

Transforms invisible effort into visible progress. Completion percentages and learning streaks give students the sense of forward movement that sustains motivation — something entirely absent when using disconnected tools.

Attendance Monitoring

Brings attendance — typically managed through a completely separate university system — into the same space as assignments. Students can identify attendance gaps early and connect them to their academic strategy, instead of discovering issues only when it's too late.

Settings and Personalization

Allows students to tailor the experience to their specific academic context — whether that's adjusting notification preferences, managing subject lists, or configuring how progress is measured. Personalization increases long-term adoption by making the tool feel like their own.

Impact

How this helps students

Clarity is a conceptual project, but it was designed with real outcomes in mind. Based on the problems identified in research and the design decisions made, the expected impact is:

  • Fewer missed deadlines — A centralized, always-visible assignment list with status indicators removes the "out of sight, out of mind" failure mode that causes students to miss work.
  • Reduced daily cognitive load — By eliminating the need to context-switch across 3–5 tools, students spend less mental energy on information management and more on the actual work.
  • Better self-awareness — Progress tracking and attendance monitoring give students an accurate picture of where they stand, enabling more informed decisions about how to prioritize their time.
  • Lower stress around academic responsibilities — Visibility reduces uncertainty. When students can see their situation clearly, they feel more in control, which research links directly to reduced academic anxiety.

After sharing the concept with students, the centralized dashboard was seen as the most valuable feature — not because it adds new capabilities, but because it eliminates the need to switch between LMS portals, messaging groups, and personal notes just to get a basic overview.

The goal was never to build a feature-rich platform. It was to make students feel less overwhelmed and more in control of their academic lives.

Learnings

What I learned

  • Visibility reduces stress more effectively than reminders alone
  • A strong information hierarchy solves more problems than complex UI
  • Designing systems is very different from designing individual screens
  • Consistency across pages builds trust in a product
  • Problem-first thinking leads to better design decisions than jumping into visuals
